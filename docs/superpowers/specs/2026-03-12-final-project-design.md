# DATASCI 185 final project design

## Summary

The final group project (20% of the final grade) asks students to propose an AI application for a real-world problem and audit existing tools in the same space. Groups of 4-5 first-year students pick a domain from a curated list, test 2-3 existing AI tools, then design their own application with a harms assessment. The project is non-technical: no programming required, though an optional stretch component lets groups build a simple prototype with no-code tools for bonus credit.

## Format

- Groups of 4-5 students
- 8 curated domains to choose from
- Two submissions: optional proposal + graded final deliverable
- Final deliverable: ~5-page report (PDF or Word) + 1-page infographic (PDF)
- Optional "Build it" appendix for bonus points

## Domains

1. Healthcare: symptom checking, mental health support, medical image interpretation
2. Education: AI tutoring, automated grading, personalised learning
3. Criminal justice: predictive policing, risk assessment, facial recognition
4. Hiring and employment: CV screening, interview analysis, workforce analytics
5. Creative industries: AI-generated art/music/writing, content moderation
6. Finance: credit scoring, fraud detection, algorithmic trading
7. Environment and climate: weather forecasting, energy optimisation, biodiversity monitoring
8. Misinformation and media: deepfake detection, fact-checking, recommendation algorithms

Groups within the same domain must address different use cases. Domain selection is first-come-first-served via a sign-up sheet on Canvas. If a domain is saturated (i.e., it becomes hard to find distinct use cases), the instructor may ask latecomers to pick another domain.

## Deliverables

### Submission 1: project proposal (optional, strongly recommended)

Due April 1. One page, three paragraphs:

1. Domain and use case: which domain, what problem, why it matters
2. Existing tools: 2-3 AI tools they plan to test and why
3. Proposed application: brief sketch of the AI application they intend to design

Instructor provides written feedback. Not graded.

### Submission 2: final deliverable (graded)

Due April 27 at 11:59 PM. Two items:

**Report (~5 pages, PDF or Word):**

1. Introduction: the problem and why AI is relevant
2. Landscape review: description and testing of 2-3 existing AI tools, with documented interactions (screenshots, sample prompts/outputs)
3. Proposed application: what they would build, who it is for, what data it needs, how it works (conceptual, no code)
4. Harms assessment: potential biases, privacy risks, failure modes, mitigation strategies
5. Conclusion: summary and reflection

**Infographic (1 page, PDF):**

Visual summary of the project: the problem, proposed solution, and key risks. Any tool (Canva, PowerPoint, etc.) is fine.

### Optional: "Build it" appendix

Not counted toward the 5-page limit. Groups build a simple prototype using no-code tools (custom GPT, Poe chatbot, visual RAG workflow, prompt chain). Documented with screenshots and a brief explanation. Worth up to 1 bonus point: 1 point for a working prototype with clear documentation; 0.5 for a partial attempt or minimal documentation.

## Grading rubric (10 points + 1 bonus)

| Component | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| Introduction | 1 |
| Landscape review | 2 |
| Proposed application | 3 |
| Harms assessment | 2 |
| Infographic | 1 |
| Writing quality (includes conclusion) | 1 |
| Bonus: technical stretch | +1 |

## Group formation

Students have until March 23 to self-select into groups of 4-5. Any student without a group by March 25 is assigned randomly by the instructor. Groups formed by random assignment are especially encouraged to submit the optional proposal, since they will have had less time to coordinate.

## Design decisions

- **10-point scale**: simpler numbers, multiplied by final weight for grade calculation
- **Optional proposal**: reduces pressure on first-years while still encouraging early engagement
- **Landscape review weighted at 2, proposed application at 3**: the design/creative component gets more weight, matching the course emphasis on design thinking
- **No live presentations**: with ~26 groups, presentations would consume multiple class sessions
- **No-code stretch component**: accessible to non-technical students, rewards initiative without penalising those who skip it
- **Syllabus updates needed**: change group size from 3-4 to 4-5; replace "demo video" with "infographic"; update project description text
